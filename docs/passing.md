# Passing and receiving

Directional selection uses a 30-degree cone and excludes dismissed players.
The chosen recipient is retained from release through actual foot contact;
movement during the wind-up does not silently assign a different teammate.
The HUD names a candidate while charging.

Ground delivery starts at the actual ball position, estimates rolling drag and
leads a moving receiver within a reachable distance. Charge
scales the delivery around its distance-calibrated strength, preserving weak
and overhit passes. Through passes lead into attacking space; lobs solve a
free physical arc for the intended distance. Humans and AI use the same solver.

Reception predicts the physical ball trajectory rather than chasing its current
position. A moving receiving foot applies the cushion only on swept contact.
There is no snapping, tether or guaranteed completion, and opponents can
intercept. Both feet remain eligible. The receiving assignment ends at the
first controlled touch. A brief receiving recovery lets the player stabilize
before choosing a shot, pass or advancing touch. The last passer is remembered
for a possible return combination, assessed against available space and defenders.
The recovery uses a planted stopping touch and braking intent. It does not let
ordinary carry intent push the ball away while merely blocking another kick.

The team coordinator assigns one recipient for each valid delivery. The passer's
capsule being near the outgoing ball does not take that job away. Other players
retain passing support and defensive cover; equivalent open runs persist rather
than changing every decision. Interceptions and actual receiving touches release
the assignment.

Before an accepted kick makes contact, its passer retains the preparation job
and the intended teammate opens or waits at the selected delivery area. The
recipient starts intercepting the live delivery only after actual foot contact.
Misses and interceptions release that reservation; proximity to a departing
pass still cannot reclaim it. A retained AI pass plan reserves the same jobs
while braking and finding its stance, before a physical kick is accepted.

AI sideways and backward passes use a physical kicking stance. A committed
recipient remains selected while the passer steps beside and behind the ball;
automatic dribble touches pause during this setup. A moving ball is first
stopped through an ordinary physical
foot touch, then automatic touches pause while the player steps into position.
An accepted kick snapshots
its recipient, and no repeated decision rewrites it during the wind-up. A lost
ball, ineligible recipient or blocked preparation cancels the plan. Bodies and
ball collisions remain active throughout.
